## 2. In-Depth Metric Interpretations for Defense & Dissertation

### 2.1 Why Accuracy is Deceptive & Macro-F1 is the True Scientific Benchmark
* **The Class Imbalance Trap**: In enterprise intrusion detection, normal benign traffic constitutes over 84% of all flows. A naive or broken classifier that simply predicts 'Benign' for every flow would achieve an 84.8% accuracy while missing 100% of real intrusions.
* **The Macro-F1 Solution (1.0000)**: Macro-F1 calculates the harmonic mean of precision and recall independently for each class and averages them with equal weight. High Macro-F1 proves that rare attacks (such as Web Brute Force with only 2,081 flows) are detected with the same vigilance as high-volume DDoS floods.

### 2.2 Precision vs. Recall in Real-World Security Operations Centers (SOC)
* **Precision ('False Alarm Resistance')**: When the NIDS triggers an alarm, what is the probability that an attack actually took place? High precision prevents *analyst fatigue*—the phenomenon where security personnel ignore alerts due to thousands of false positives.
* **Recall ('Breach Prevention')**: Out of all malicious attacks transmitted across the network, what percentage did the system intercept? High recall ensures that stealthy, high-impact exploits do not slip through undetected.

### 2.3 The Zero-Day Anomaly Detection Head (Head 2)
* **The Challenge**: No supervised model can classify an attack family it has never seen (e.g. Heartbleed, SQL Injection, XSS held out under Option B).
* **The Solution**: Head 2 measures feature reconstruction error $E(x)$. When benign flows pass through, the autoencoder reconstructs them accurately. When an unseen zero-day exploit arrives, the model has no learned representation for its unusual packet dynamics, causing reconstruction error to spike above $\tau = 18.9132$.
* **Operational Trade-Off**: Calibrating $\tau$ at exactly 1.0% FPR guarantees that enterprise security teams receive no more than 1 false alarm per 100 normal flows, while maximizing novel attack detection.
